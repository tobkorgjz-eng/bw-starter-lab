# Client intake questions

Ask these in order. Write the client's own words where you can. If an answer
is not known, write `unknown — ask again by <date>`.

## 1. The business

1. What is the business called, and what does it do, in one or two sentences?
2. Where does it operate (address, area served, online only)?
3. What are the opening hours? Are there seasonal changes?
4. How many people work there, and who does what?
5. Who are the usual customers?

## 2. People and contacts

1. Who is the owner, or the person who gives the final yes?
2. Who else can give a yes if the owner is away? For which kinds of task?
3. How should the assistant reach the person who gives a yes (email, text, chat)?
   How quickly do they usually answer?
4. Who should Blade Works talk to about the setup itself?

## 3. Work today

1. Walk me through a normal day. Where do customer messages come in
   (phone, email, website form, social media, booking system)?
2. Which tasks take the most time each week?
3. Which tasks get missed or done late?
4. What tools and accounts does the business use now (calendar, email,
   booking, payments, spreadsheets)? Names only — no passwords.

## 4. What the assistant should help with

1. Which two or three tasks would you most like help with first?
2. For each one: what does "done well" look like?
3. What should the assistant say if it does not know an answer?
4. What tone should it use with customers (formal, friendly, short)?
   Are there words or phrases to avoid?

## 5. Rules: alone, ask first, never

For each task named so far, and for the examples below, ask:
"Can the assistant do this alone, must it ask you first, or must it never do it?"
If the client is unsure, put it under **ask first**.

1. **May do alone.** Which tasks can the assistant do without asking you?
   (Examples: read incoming messages, draft a reply for review, answer
   questions from an approved FAQ, look up free calendar slots, write a
   daily summary.)
2. **Needs your yes.** Which tasks must it ask you about first and wait for
   a clear yes? (Examples: send an email or text to a customer, book or move
   an appointment, give a refund or discount, order supplies, post on
   social media, change a price.)
3. **Never does.** Which tasks must it never do, even if someone asks?
   (Examples: delete records, share passwords or login details, move money,
   agree to a contract, give anyone more access, share a customer's private
   details.)
4. Are there amounts or limits that change the answer? (For example: "a
   refund under 20 needs my yes; over 20 it never does — I handle it myself.")
5. Are there times when the rules change (after hours, holidays, when you
   are away)?
6. How should the assistant ask for a yes, and what should it do if no
   answer comes (wait, remind, or drop the task)?

## 6. Information the assistant may use

1. What facts should it know (prices, services, policies, FAQ)?
   Where are they written down today?
2. Is any information private and off limits to the assistant?
3. How long should messages, drafts and logs be kept?

## 7. Success and next steps

1. In a month, how will you judge whether the assistant is helping?
2. Is there anything that worries you about using an assistant?
3. When should we review these answers together?
