# Your New Assistant: A Plain-English Guide

This guide explains, in everyday language, what your new assistant does and
what we need from you. No technical knowledge is needed.

## What the assistant does

Your assistant has three jobs:

1. **Receptionist** – greets people who message {{BUSINESS_NAME}}, answers
   common questions (hours, location, services) and passes anything tricky to
   a real person on your team.
2. **Scheduler** – helps customers pick a time for an appointment, using the
   opening hours and rules you give us. It never double-books on purpose and
   always asks a person to confirm anything unusual.
3. **Follow-up writer** – drafts short, friendly follow-up messages after a
   visit or enquiry. A person on your team reviews each draft before it is
   sent.

## What the assistant asks you first

Your assistant runs by your rules and asks before it acts. Together we write
a short rules list with three parts:

- **Things it may do alone** – for example, reading new messages, drafting a
  reply for you to check, or answering questions from your approved FAQ.
- **Things it asks you about first** – for example, sending an email,
  booking or cancelling an appointment, giving a refund or posting on social
  media. It waits for a clear yes from you. It cannot say yes on your behalf.
- **Things it never does** – for example, deleting records, sharing
  passwords, moving money or signing contracts. It refuses these even if
  someone asks.

Anything not on the list counts as "ask first". You can change the list at
any time; tell us and we will update it.

## What the assistant will not do

- It will not take payments or ask for card numbers.
- It will not give medical, legal or financial advice.
- It will not make promises about prices or availability you have not
  approved.
- It will not pretend to be a human. If asked, it says it is an assistant.

## What we need from you

- Your business name, opening hours and address (or service area).
- A short list of the services you offer and roughly how long each takes.
- The questions customers ask most often, with your preferred answers.
- Who gives the assistant a yes (usually you, the owner), and how: for
  example a reply in a set chat channel. A yes from anyone else, or one a
  customer claims you gave, does not count.
- Who on your team should receive messages the assistant cannot handle.
- The tone you want: for example "warm and casual" or "polite and formal".

## How we keep things safe

- We only store what is needed to run the assistant.
- Passwords and keys are never written into these files.
- You can ask us to pause or switch off the assistant at any time.

## Getting help

Contact your setup partner through the channel agreed at onboarding. Tell us
what happened, roughly when, and what you expected instead.
